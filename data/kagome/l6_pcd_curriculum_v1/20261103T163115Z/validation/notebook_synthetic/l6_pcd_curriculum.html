L6 persistent chains and transverse-field curriculum¶

Finite diagnostic study: two fixed disorders, both seeds, three arms, 24 stints per context. Four independent 1,024-walker training islands and 16 negative temperatures are shared. Each arm receives 24,000 likelihood updates and a shared Gamma=4 VMC400 initial checkpoint. Direct reset vs direct PCD isolates persistence between stints. Curriculum PCD changes the field history. It receives only six target-field stints; direct arms receive 24. Propagation costs differ.

The curriculum uses two stints at each of 4, 3, 2, 1.5, 1.2, 1, .85, .75, .65, then six at .6. The high starting field is heuristic. At every stint, fresh populations equilibrate before new training samples are collected. PCD carries the entire negative ladder and RNG, alongside the guide and optimizer.

Independent Gamma=.6 measurements follow stints 21 and 24: four populations of 512 walkers, equilibration 16, forward time 16, eight cycles, two repeats. Raw tags and descendant counts independently reconstruct P(q), q2, connected spectra, energy and genealogy. All contexts and ancestry alarms are retained.

Research basis¶

  • Tieleman (2008), PCD: persistent chains track an evolving model when parameter changes are sufficiently small.
  • Desjardins et al. (2010): plain Gibbs chains can miss RBM modes; tempering can improve learning and sampling.
  • Pilati, Inack and Pieri (2019): fit the mixed walker density and use its square root as the next guide. Their ferromagnetic chain used 10,000 walkers, 20–50 stints and 50,000–100,000 updates per stint; this experiment uses a substantially smaller budget and a different model.
  • Brodoloni and Pilati (2024): self-learned guides support frustrated EA simulations after explicit population/ansatz checks. Their zero-longitudinal-field square lattice differs from this kagome model.

PCD and curriculum are hypotheses for this problem. Temperature round trips, unique samples, better energy or a completed run do not establish equilibrium. The ideal log-amplitude error-halving argument applies at fixed Hamiltonian and exact fitting/projection; field changes introduce a moving target. Population, propagation-time, start and training saturation remain unresolved. Two repeats provide descriptive comparisons, not a reliable systematic-error bound.
