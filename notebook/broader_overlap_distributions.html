Broader overlap distributions at fixed $h=0.15$¶

These are measured pure GFMC overlap histograms for periodic kagome $L=4$ ($N=48$). Only complete ensembles with eight disorders and two repeats per disorder are shown. The same disorder IDs 60000–60007 have equal weight at every field and for both guides; no disorder is selected by its observed broadness. The earlier exact $L=2$ and guide-MCMC comparisons remain available in the companion report.

The Hamiltonian is $$H=\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}K_{ij}Z_iZ_j-0.15\sum_iZ_i-\Gamma\sum_iX_i,$$ with iid antiferromagnetic bonds $K_{ij}\sim U(0.5,1.5)$, stored as $J_{ij}=-K_{ij}$. NumPy default_rng(SeedSequence([20261027,L,sample_id,1])) draws bonds in the periodic lattice bond-list order. Quenched bonds are reused across all fields, guides, and seeds for each size and disorder ID.

Each run uses four independently branching populations with 512 walkers each, equilibration and forward times 16, and eight cycles. Descendant-weighted configurations are pooled equally across cycles; all six cross-population pairs give $q=N^{-1}\sum_i s_i t_i$. Repeats are averaged within each disorder first. The higher-field histograms are deterministic replays of frozen production trajectories. Their $q,q^2$, and connected spectra must match production to $10^{-12}$. The $\Gamma=0.60,0.65$ histograms reuse the earlier verified measurements.

$P(q)$ denotes probability mass at each allowed overlap; connecting lines are visual aids. Capped bars give $\pm s_J/\sqrt8$ pointwise in $q$. Width bars use a delete-one-disorder jackknife. These errors exclude mixing and projection bias. All ancestry alarms retain equal disorder weight. The projections remain preliminary pending population, forward-time and starting-state sensitivity. Broadening alone does not establish glassiness, a transition, or finite-size drift. Computation, checks, plotting and notebook execution take place on AutoDL.

def width(a):
    mean=a.mean(0)
    return np.sqrt(max(0,float(q*q@mean-(q@mean)**2)))
def jackknife(a,stat):
    x=np.array([stat(np.delete(a,i,axis=0)) for i in range(8)])
    return float(np.sqrt(7/8*np.sum((x-x.mean())**2)))

The following diagnostics use the same histograms and raw descendant measurements. The width is that of the disorder-averaged distribution. Its variance splits into the average within-disorder variance and the variance of disorder means; this distinguishes changes within samples from a spread of sample centers. Uniform magnetization and negative-overlap mass are displayed alongside width because the fixed longitudinal field breaks spin-inversion symmetry.
